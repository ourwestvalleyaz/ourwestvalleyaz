## What a yes or no vote means

{{< ballot-comparison >}}

{{< ballot-choice vote="yes" >}}

**Approves the permanent base adjustment.**

- Increases Tolleson's 1979-80 expenditure base by **$39,033,506**.
- Establishes a new expenditure base of **$40,000,000**.
- The base continues to be adjusted annually for population growth and inflation.
- The adjusted expenditure limitation begins in **fiscal year 2028**.
- According to the City's ballot language, Tolleson would be permitted to spend revenues it already receives, subject to the annual budget process.

{{< /ballot-choice >}}

{{< ballot-choice vote="no" >}}

**Does not approve the permanent base adjustment.**

- The proposed **$39,033,506** adjustment is not made.
- According to the City, Tolleson would continue operating under its current expenditure-limitation method for **two additional years**.
- If the City does not obtain an alternative expenditure limitation in **2028**, it would be subject to the state-imposed expenditure limit.
- Under that limit, the City could not spend revenues beyond the amount permitted by the state formula.

{{< /ballot-choice >}}

## Why Proposition 416 is on the ballot

Arizona's expenditure-limit formula uses municipal spending from fiscal year 1979-80 as its base and adjusts that amount for population growth and inflation. Changing the base requires voter approval.

Tolleson currently operates under a voter-approved Home Rule expenditure limitation. According to the City's FAQ, the current Home Rule was approved in 2024 and must be approved by voters every four years. A permanent base adjustment instead changes the base used by the state expenditure-limit formula and, once approved, remains in effect permanently.

The Tolleson City Council submitted the permanent base adjustment to voters through Resolution No. 2635. The City states that it is seeking the adjustment because Tolleson's population and service demands have grown while the state formula continues to use fiscal year 1979-80 as its baseline.

## What Proposition 416 would change

Arizona's state expenditure limitation for cities and towns uses a base tied to fiscal year 1979-80 and adjusts that amount over time for population growth and inflation.

Proposition 416 would permanently increase Tolleson's 1979-80 expenditure base by $39,033,506, resulting in a new base of $40,000,000.

That $40 million figure is **not Tolleson's annual budget and is not a proposed annual spending amount**. It is the revised base that would be used in the constitutional expenditure-limit calculation. The base would continue to be adjusted annually for population growth and inflation.

If approved by voters in 2026, the adjusted expenditure limitation would take effect beginning in fiscal year 2028.

The two expenditure-limit estimates apply to different fiscal years and should not be treated as competing figures for the same period.

They also should not be confused with Tolleson's adopted annual budget. Tolleson's adopted fiscal year 2026-27 budget totals **$247,153,697**. That budget is separate from the expenditure limitation, which determines how much revenue may be spent under the applicable constitutional framework. The City Council separately makes spending decisions through the annual budget process.
