Avondale Police Department records document several technologies used to collect, search, share, or review information for law-enforcement purposes. These include license plate reader cameras, police drones, body-worn cameras, and regional data-sharing systems.

This page separates what City records **currently document** from proposals or details that have **not yet been verified**.

## License plate readers

### Current system documented by the City

The FY2027 City Manager's Recommended Budget states that Avondale Police Department operates **15 license plate reader (LPR) cameras throughout the city**.

The document says officers and dispatchers use the cameras to identify and query vehicles associated with crimes and calls for service.

It also states that the camera database allows personnel to search for vehicles through cameras shared by **more than 140 law-enforcement agencies nationwide**.

The City describes real-time alerts associated with categories including missing persons, vehicles used in felonies, stolen vehicles, stolen license plates, and other law-enforcement alerts.

### Expansion and Flock Safety agreements

Avondale's FY2027 recommended budget requested **58 additional license plate reader cameras** to expand coverage into areas without existing cameras. The tentative budget also listed a **$239,000 ongoing funding request** for this expansion.

### How long is ALPR information retained?

The original Flock order form specifies a **30-day retention period**. The agreement also addresses rolling deletion and the City's ability to export or separately archive information.

The 30-day setting therefore does not establish that every copy of a record is permanently deleted after 30 days.

Source: Request 26-1207, *24-004C - PSA - Flock Safety.pdf*, pp. 17 and 22.

### Searching and sharing vehicle information

The FlockOS product description includes vehicle searches by plate, location, time, and identifying characteristics. It also describes alerts, hot lists, community camera access, and statewide or nationwide lookup and sharing capabilities.

These are features described in the agreement. **The released contracts do not independently establish which optional features or sharing arrangements Avondale has activated.**

The agreement also describes Flock ownership of its hardware.

Source: Request 26-1207, *24-004C - PSA - Flock Safety.pdf*, pp. 19 and 34.

## Earlier LPR history

Avondale's documented use of license plate reader technology predates the current program.

A historical City budget describes implementation and training for an LPR system around 2008. The document says external cameras could read and store license plates and reports that approximately **50,000 plates had been collected** during an early deployment period.

The FY2023 final budget later reported that the Police Department had **launched a license plate reader program** and used it to identify two stolen vehicles.

Because older City records already document LPR use, OWVAZ does not interpret the FY2023 wording as evidence that FY2023 was Avondale's first-ever use of license plate readers.

The FY2025 final budget also describes testing cameras functioning as license plate readers at intersections. The City says these cameras capture an image of vehicles passing through an intersection and can notify police when a vehicle of interest passes a monitored location.

## Police drones

Avondale had an established police drone program before the FY2027 budget cycle.

The FY2022 final budget documents a new drone program and says seven staff members were trained as licensed drone pilots.

The FY2023 final budget reports that two additional drones were added.

By the FY2027 recommended budget, the Police Department described a program consisting of **16 drones** assigned to officers who had completed FAA training and received drone pilot licenses.

## Drone as First Responder

The FY2027 recommended budget describes a proposed **Drone as First Responder (DFR)** model.

The City's description involves a drone stored in an enclosed rooftop docking station that could be deployed by trained personnel to calls for service.

The document says the system could provide information to responding officers before or during their arrival.

The FY2027 supplemental-request summary lists a **$120,000 one-time Public Safety Drone Program** request.

### Flock drone equipment and services

The March 2026 Flock amendment includes drone-response technology. The listed products include Aerodome radar, an M4TD drone and Dock 3 package, and Flock911 integration.

The equipment descriptions establish what was contracted, not whether the systems are currently flying or whether FAA approvals were issued.

Source: Request 26-1207, *24-004C - First Amendment - Flock.pdf*, pp. 7–9 and 11–13.

### Who owns the drone equipment and related information?

The drone-services addendum states that Flock retains ownership of its hardware.

The agreement also distinguishes **Customer Data** from certain **Flock Drone IP**, which can include flight logs, telemetry, radar-derived information, connected-device information, and aggregated usage data.

Flock receives certain contractual rights to create and market analysis or insights derived from the information covered by those provisions.

This distinction matters when evaluating who controls information generated by the technology. The contract does not, by itself, establish that Flock owns every image recorded for Avondale or sells identifiable police surveillance footage.

Source: Request 26-1207, *24-004C - First Amendment - Flock.pdf*, pp. 9–10.

## Body-worn cameras

Avondale Police Department's command-staff page explicitly refers to an **Axon Body Worn Camera Program**.

This establishes Axon in connection with Avondale's body-worn-camera program.

It does **not** establish that Axon supplies Avondale's license plate readers or drone systems.

FY2027 budget materials also contain a separate **$25,000 ongoing request for body-worn-camera redaction software**.

## Regional and interagency data sharing

The FY2026 final budget reports that Avondale assigned **one full-time dispatcher to the West Valley Real-Time Crime Center**.

Separately, the FY2027 recommended budget describes a **Police Data Sharing** request of:

- **$75,000 ongoing**
- **$28,000 one-time**
- **$103,000 total**

The City describes the proposed system as a nationwide data-integration platform intended to combine otherwise separate law-enforcement records into a searchable system and support interagency information sharing.

OWVAZ does not currently treat the Police Data Sharing system, the West Valley Real-Time Crime Center, and the 140-plus-agency LPR camera database as the same system. The public records establish these facts separately.

## Flock Safety procurement and contract costs

The City's 2026 expansion purchase order totals **$303,490**, including $180,000 for ALPR equipment, $100,000 for drone-response equipment and services, and $23,490 in sales tax.

The March 2026 amendment separately lists a **$475,000 net annual recurring contractual amount**.

These are different financial measures, not proof of actual payments. The related purchase order and invoice should not be counted as separate purchases.

The [Avondale FY2027 budget page](/communities/avondale/budgets/fy2027/#notable-spending) includes the historical purchase orders, recurring contract rate, and document references.

Sources: Request 26-1207, *Avondale PO - 22700912_N.pdf*, p. 1; *24-004C - First Amendment - Flock.pdf*, p. 8.

## What the public record does not yet show

### Camera locations and deployment status

On October 1, 2026, OWVAZ requested Avondale's ALPR inventory, camera locations, and supporting agreements. The City released ten PDF documents comprising 71 pages and closed request **26-1207** on October 14.

Although the agreements cover 73 ALPR units, **the released documents did not identify individual camera locations or establish how many units are currently operating**.

This does not establish that the City lacks location records or intentionally withheld them.

### Other unanswered questions

The records reviewed do not establish:

- Avondale's complete ALPR access and audit policies or its specific enabled interagency-sharing agreements.
- Which additional ALPR units have been installed or activated.
- Whether the contracted drone-response systems are operating or have obtained the necessary FAA authorizations.
- The exact vendor or product associated with the separate FY2027 Police Data Sharing request.
- The actual payments made under the Flock purchase orders and invoices.

The FY2027 budget separately identifies a $239,000 State RICO funding assignment. Its similarity to the earlier $239,000 camera funding request does not establish that the final appropriation paid for the Flock expansion.
