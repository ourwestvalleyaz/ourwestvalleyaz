## Accuracy, sources, interpretation, and verification

OWVAZ takes accuracy seriously. Information is researched and organized
using public records, official government sources, and other authoritative
sources whenever practical. Important factual claims are expected to be
supported by identifiable evidence.

OWVAZ may summarize, organize, categorize, compare, or explain public records
and other source material. Those explanations are not themselves official
government records and should be read together with the identified sources
when the distinction matters.

Reasonable people can sometimes interpret the same record or collection of
records differently. OWVAZ aims to distinguish documented facts from
interpretation and to identify material uncertainty when the available
evidence does not establish a conclusion. AI output is not treated as an
authoritative source.

Public information can also change after publication. When a current
deadline, legal requirement, eligibility determination, or other
time-sensitive official information matters, residents should confirm the
current information with the responsible agency or authoritative source.

## Proposals, budgets, and public records

OWVAZ distinguishes between what public records document and what those
records do not establish. A proposal, budget request, appropriation, planned
purchase, or similar record does not by itself prove that a project,
purchase, policy, system, or deployment was ultimately completed or put into
use.

When the available public record does not establish a fact, OWVAZ aims to
say so rather than fill the gap with an assumption.

## Completeness

OWVAZ works to identify and explain useful public information, but the site
may not contain every relevant record, update, event, agency action, or
development.

The absence of information from OWVAZ should not by itself be treated as
evidence that an event, action, record, purchase, decision, or other
development did not occur.
